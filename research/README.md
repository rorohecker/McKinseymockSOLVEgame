# Research folder

Everything here was gathered on **2026-10-08** from public web pages. Notes are paraphrased and structured; raw pages are not copied (copyright, and the pages change). Follow the URLs in `SOURCES.md` for originals.

## How claims are labelled

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **OFFICIAL** | Stated on a McKinsey page or in McKinsey's own FAQ PDF. |
| **CORROBORATED** | Reported by 3+ separate sources. Note that prep vendors often cite each other, so this is weaker than it sounds. |
| **REPORTED** | Reported by 1-2 sources (candidates, coaches or one vendor). |
| **VENDOR CLAIM** | A prep provider's own claim about its product or its users. Treat as marketing. |
| **CONTRADICTED** | Sources disagree. See `DISCREPANCIES.md`. |
| **UNVERIFIED** | Seen once, no support, or the source itself says it is unproven. |

## What is *not* known

McKinsey publishes **no** scoring formula, pass mark, weighting between games, or full game specification. Anything numeric about scoring comes from candidates or vendor simulators. Wherever this project's simulator uses a number, `docs/KNOWN_DEVIATIONS.md` says where it came from.

## Re-verifying

Pages change often (the 95-minute invite and the redesigned SFL are described as pilots). Before relying on a number, open the URL in `SOURCES.md`. The invitation email and in-game tutorial always take precedence over any guide.

## One site was left out on purpose

`solvegamesguide.com` carries a notice asking AI agents not to summarise or reuse its content. Its content is not used in these notes. Its existence and its free tier are noted only through a third-party comparison (CaseStar).
